Motivation Is Not Enough

Motivation is important, but only as an impetus; it gets you moving toward a goal, but it doesn’t last. Constancy and consistency is the key to acquiring knowledge.

Written by Peter Mangiaracina

“Motivate” is a transitive verb, meaning someone else can do it to you, as in “I motivate you.” Nevertheless, motivation is really something you have to do to yourself. What’s worse, it disappears quickly, so it must constantly be renewed.

When it comes from a necessity, for example, when you must learn a language or some other skill for work, you’re highly motivated from the negative consequence of losing your job. When you want to learn a language because a family member married into a family from another country, you might be highly motivated because you want to communicate with his or her spouse as well as with their children. In that case you’re motivated from the affirmative consequence of family participation.

When you embark on learning something new, it is important to have a goal and constantly remind yourself of that goal. Sadly, though, it’s not enough.

I’ve noticed from working as an English professor for 30 years that motivation flags at about three months even though most of my students need English for their jobs. I know this because that’s when people start showing up to class late, begin to cancel classes instead of rescheduling them, and start looking at their watches half way through class. It’s annoying as hell, but it’s just human nature.

So what can you do when motivation is fickle and ephemeral? Routine. If you want to do something that takes a long time, you need to make it a part of your life, like taking a shower, brushing your teeth, and setting your alarm. Always make a schedule and stick to it. Organize your life around that time you carve out. My students, for example, always come twice a week for an hour at a specific time of day. Once their motivation to learn flags, the schedule is in place and it’s more likely they will continue taking classes and meet their goals.

Motivation is important, but only as an impetus; it gets you moving toward a goal, but it doesn’t last. Constancy and consistency are the keys to acquiring knowledge.
