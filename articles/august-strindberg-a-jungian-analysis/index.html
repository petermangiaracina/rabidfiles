Rabid File / Psychology & Philosophy

August Strindberg:
A Jungian Analysis

A psychological chamber play drawn from a college personality-theory project

Written by Peter Mangiaracina

Editorial note

This presentation sets the original college paper as a chamber play. The introduction, session dialogue, analyst’s notes, and sources have been retained as distinct parts of the work. The session itself is an imaginative reconstruction; Jung and Strindberg never met.

Personality Theory Project

INTRODUCTION

In 1895, at the time Carl Jung was undergoing medical training and qualification at Basel University in Switzerland, August Strindberg, famous and infamous in his home country of Sweden for his powerful and misogynistic plays and tracts, was suffering the first of what would be five psychotic episodes he called his “Inferno”.

He never succumbed to psychosis. But he was never cured. For the rest of his life he was plagued by persecution mania, his relationships were crippled by jealousy, and he was always set apart by his genius.

Strindberg and Jung were barely contemporaries. There was never a meeting between these two men; in fact, Strindberg was never hospitalized for his disorder and never consulted a psychiatrist. Every subsequent diagnosis has been pure speculation based almost completely on inferences drawn from his plays and autobiographical works.

The following is a series of session transcripts and notes that could have been made by Carl Jung had he worked with August Strindberg. Strindberg’s words are a condensation of many different events he chronicled in his autobiography “Inferno” which I have dramatized for the purposes of this project.

Dramatis Personae

CARL JUNG — A young Swiss physician in training, methodical and observant.

AUGUST STRINDBERG — Swedish naturalist playwright, alchemist, visionary, and reluctant patient.

Analyst’s Notes · 1:30

Appointment with August Strindberg

Swedish. Naturalist playwright. Complains of persecution. Generalizes to his entire country. Physical manifestations: constant references to sensations of electrical shock. Experiments with alchemy and the occult.

Tone & Theme of dramas — Pessimism and Misogyny: the emotional world is violent and unpredictable, peopled by male victims and their female counterparts/tormenters.

Session No. 1

THE DISTRACTED PATIENT

JUNG

…We can talk more about your family a little later. You seem rather distracted.

STRINDBERG

(pause)

Yes. Perhaps I am a little distracted.

(pause)

And very tired.

JUNG

I understood that you were to arrive last night and stay at an hotel here in Zurich. Was there a change in plans?

STRINDBERG

(pause)

No. I did in fact arrive last night. And I did stay at an hotel in Zurich. But I did not sleep.

JUNG

Why not?

SILENCE

JUNG

Were you wound up from the trip?

SILENCE

(Strindberg scratches a scab on his hand.)

JUNG

Nervous about our meeting?

STRINDBERG

Of course not, no.

SILENCE

STRINDBERG

(looks up at the ceiling and out the window, then at his hands. Sighs. Hears something)

What was that sound?

JUNG

You heard something?

STRINDBERG

Who are your neighbors?

Analyst’s Notes

After Session No. 1

Taciturn. He remained silent for most of the session and asked questions that seemed to pertain to his personal security. Continually looked at his hands. Hands covered with scars from what looks like chemical burns. Looked over his shoulders. The ancient Greeks refer to “the Furies” that chase the doomed hero, his fate waiting to catch up to him. What is the face of his furie?

Session No. 9

SIRI

JUNG

Tell me more about your relationship with your first wife, Siri.

STRINDBERG

Siri. I never adored a woman more than I did her. And never met one with more of a gift for seduction! She was the cause of all the misfortunes of my life.

JUNG

How?

STRINDBERG

(shakes his head thoughtfully)

I fell in love with her the moment I met her, but not as a man loves a woman. More like the love one has for an angel: chaste, unspoiled. I was the son of a maidservant and a bankrupt wretch. She was high above me in social rank, the wife of a baron.

JUNG

How did you meet?

STRINDBERG

She and the baron had seen my plays and liked them. They invited me to dinner once, twice, three times. I became a regular at their house.

(At the word “house” he becomes uncomfortable, shifts in his chair, looks over his shoulder, then at the ceiling.)

JUNG

What about the house?

STRINDBERG

It was an omen.

JUNG

An Omen?

STRINDBERG

Of what was to come. I spent the unhappiest years of my childhood there, at that very address in Norrtullsgartan. History was about to repeat itself. But I was blinded by worship.

JUNG

Siri?

STRINDBERG

Yes. So many years an atheist and finally this latent need for an object of worship surfaced with…such intensity. The void inside me was suddenly filled. God is a woman, or so I thought, both virgin and mother. The world had always seemed unpleasant and boring, peopled by mean-spirited biological accidents. Suddenly, everything changed. I had something to adore. I felt as if I were seeing everything for the first time: trees, flowers, horizons.

JUNG

And the baron? Did he know about your feelings for Siri?

STRINDBERG

The baron was having an affair with his cousin. He tried to make me his confidant, but I put him off. Looking back, I realize that he may have orchestrated our affair to ease his own guilt.

JUNG

When did your feelings for her become sexual?

STRINDBERG

She came back unexpectedly from a trip to the country with her daughter. The baron was enraged with her, and she departed again that very day. When I called on her she told me that the baron was angry with her for coming back early because he was looking forward to spending the weekend with his cousin. I saw my chance to cast myself in the role of confidant, but something perverse in me made me take the baron’s side against her. I said something to which she asked “Are you insulting me?”. I replied, “Yes, baroness, I am insulting you”. We argued, then I turned and ran home.

JUNG

That is when you began to see her in a different way?

STRINDBERG

Yes. In that moment I saw what schemers women were. That is the moment that my hatred of her sex was born in me. I had to take the baron’s side. She had insulted all men, not just me. I seethed with an all consuming anger like poison. Soon my anger began to overwhelm me. In my mind I thought of ways of desecrating my “madonna”. I imagined her naked, raped by beggars. I took down a book of nude sculptures from my bookshelf to find a picture that looked like Siri. I decided on Diana. I imagined her with her clothes torn off. I was burning with sensuality. Now I no longer worshiped her. I just wanted to copulate with her

Analyst’s Notes

Mother and Religion

Patient has shown strong evidence of two complexes. Data from word association tests and session dialogues seem to constellate around two complexes: Mother and Religion.

As a young boy, S was his mother’s second favorite, brother Axel taking the primal seat as Number One Son. Spurred on by jealously, S would do anything to get his mother’s attention, good or bad. Situation exacerbated by being one of eight children. Mother was pious woman and quoted bible frequently. Mother died when he was 13, in the throes of puberty, fusing the traumatic experience with sexual awakening.

His love for Siri was a regression to the worship he felt for his mother. His paroxysm of lust immediately following what would be considered a minor rift between he and his object of worship a regression to the time of her death and his awakening desire; however, this lust, in finding expression finally through the imaginary sexual domination of her, and apart from its obvious confusion with the shadow side of his power complex, signaled a progression. His libido awkwardly surged forward

The Mother archetype is the most powerful of all in Strindberg’s collective unconscious. The energy from all others seems to be drawn to it. Even the God archetype is subservient, as is power and shadow. He judges everything by the ruler of mother: good and bad, divine and damned, strong and weak, masculine and feminine, shadow and substance.

To free him of the tyranny of this requires we work on strengthening his anima, to show him her face and to free energy from the Mother to use for individuating other archetypes.

One other archetype worth mentioning is the Magician, which S associates with father love. He uses it for expression of male traits of logic and proportion.

Session No. 27

THE MAGICIAN

JUNG

When did you first get interested in alchemy and the occult?

STRINDBERG

You mean in Paris?

JUNG

No. Let’s go further back.

STRINDBERG

(waving his hand)

I’ve always been interested in science. Even as a boy I had a chemistry set.

JUNG

You were very poor. That’s quite an expense.

STRINDBERG

Yes…I was quite surprised when my father bought it for me. He seemed to encourage the idea.

JUNG

He hadn’t encouraged any other pursuits?

STRINDBERG

Neither. He seemed…alarmed by most of the things I did.

JUNG

Alarmed.

STRINDBERG

I was very good at discovering things hidden inside others. I could sit for hours discovering, shapes, faces, even landscapes in a single knot of wood. I could generate thousands of symbols from a few lines of flat poetry.

JUNG

When did this interest in chemistry surface again?

STRINDBERG

Soon after my second marriage, to Frida, disintegrated. A short time before 1895. I was alone in Paris and nearly destitute. At that time I was drinking quite a bit at a little cafe where members of an occult society of alchemists gathered. One of them was taken by some articles I had written about science for a local magazine. He got me started. Soon I was trying to synthesize gold from carbon in my hotel room. That was when I decided to give up the literary life and devote my energies to alchemy.

Analyst’s Notes

Personality Type

Giving up writing plays for a while to devote his time to alchemical experiments can be seen as an attempt at individuating the magician archetype, closely associated with his father, to get closer to his sense of animus. Note that the rekindled interest follows the end of a relationship of considerably less psychic power than his first.

At this point I’ve gathered enough information to make a preliminary decision about personality type.

Type: Introverted intuitive type, using thinking as an auxiliary.

Some ideas about this:

  • Alchemy \ magician archetype \ animus \ father \ thinking function.
  • Playwriting \ mother archetype (sub God archetype) \ anima \ intuitive function.

Science requires experimentation. Experimentation requires challenges to the logical thought process. Experiments require extensive preparations, close observation, copious and all inclusive notes and countless retrials with shifting variables. Constructing an experiment differs with how Strindberg constructs a drama. If he constructed his plays like a scientist, he would be more like his countryman, Ibsen, who believes in outlining his plays beforehand and constantly testing his dialogue in workshops. Strindberg describes his dramas as coming from “out of the blue”, a classic statement of the intuitive type. The fact that he chose Alchemy, (inward, mysterious, ritualistic, richly symbolic), rather than a “legitimate” science (outward, pragmatic, demonstrable, leaving little room for interpretation) shows the dominance of his intuition and the primitive form of his thinking function.

(Parallel: alchemy was the primitive form of chemistry)

Session No. 32

THE MACHINE

STRINDBERG

The women’s movement has built a machine to electrocute me. Last week I checked into a hotel here in Zurich. A woman wearing a brightly colored scarf, who I am sure had followed me from the station, stared me down in the lobby with a look of such disgust I thought my most recent meal would turn to sulfur.

JUNG

Was anything else significant about her?

STRINDBERG

She carried a lead case of the kind that is used to transport electrical equipment. I swallowed my fear and, in a most unassuming and gentle manner, inquired as to the purpose of her visit. The viper told me she was a social volunteer here to provide a “service” for her country.

JUNG

What was the nature of this service?

STRINDBERG

I didn’t ask. I didn’t have to! I knew she was there to execute me. Yes. I am sure that very night, as I slept in complete innocence, she and her cohorts would pass enough voltage through me to render me unrecognizable as human to the local coroner!

(he pauses. Waits for reaction, but gets none.)

When I got to my room I prepared for bed. On my night table were seven vials of mixing chemicals I had placed there moments before, and an open bible. The bible was arranged in such a way as to look random, left open by a curious or pious former guest. There on the page was a tiny red smudge. From my chemical experiments I knew immediately it was a mixture of blood and lipstick. The smudge pointed directly to Revelations 16:18:

“And there were voices and thunders, and lightnings; and there was a great earthquake such as not since men were on the earth, so mighty an earthquake, and so great.”

In that moment I knew I was marked for death. In the room above me I heard the laughter of women and little girls. In the forward apartment someone dropped something very heavy on the floor. The walls shook. I heard the sound of someone priming a gasoline engine. Why? Inside an elegant hotel in Switzerland why would anyone be starting up an engine if not to power the electrical generator to incinerate me? It was the viper from the lobby! In those few terrifying moments I became certain that my itinerary had been discovered and widely circulated amongst my enemies.

I dropped the bible and tried to phone down to the concierge for help, but my hands, shaking with fear and so badly burned by chemicals, were as useless as earlobes on a fish.

I ran out into the hall in my nightgown and, as I did, saw the figure of a woman hurrying into an apartment, slamming the door behind her. I hurriedly went past the apartment on the way to the stairs. On the floor in front of the door were several toothpicks. Anyone else would take them for evidence of careless housekeeping, nothing more, but on closer inspection I could see they were arranged to form a pentagram.

Descending the stairwell I could hear the hum of a Dynamo somewhere above me. The walls vibrated and the light bulbs over my head flickered once, twice, I think seven times. The doors to the 7th, 6th, and 5th floors were locked, I tried the 6th floor door three times, as I kept hearing the click of tumblers each time I turned to go down the stairs.

Then I smelled ozone. I had only seconds and I threw myself into the first open door I could find. I spent the rest of the evening there, in the Ladies room.

Analyst’s Notes

Dream Logic and Revelation

This psychotic episode has the vertiginous, rapid cut and pasted quality of a dream. It also features poetic irony, perhaps not worthy, but certainly indicative of a dramatist.

This is one of many episodes that took place in hotels all over Europe. In one episode (1895) he became fixed on the idea that a friend who had turned on him was in the hotel room adjacent to his. He was certain that this man was mirroring every move he made. He would sit at his desk and so would the man in the next room. He would open a letter and the neighbor would do likewise. Soon he was convinced the man was releasing poison gas through a common vent to poison him.

Hotels are large and unfamiliar places where one has no control over the common ground. This current attack was precipitated by the intense anxiety he felt in environments he could not readily manipulate.

Given this period in his life, characterized by an active interest in Swedenborg (a countryman; an occult Christian writing about many kinds of spirits resembling archetypes), and a persistent preoccupation with the bible, I would say that this episode found its theme from the open book on his night table. Moments before he had placed his chemical vials there. The open book, with which he was more than familiar, was picked up by his subconscious.

Revelations is the book of the bible that prophesies Armegeddon, or the second coming of Christ. Several of the objects he encounters are to be found in that book: the seven vials on his night table are the seven vials that the angel pours on the earth and the waters to herald Armegeddon; the shaking walls are the great quakes mentioned in the quoted passage; the lights on the stairs flickering seven times is the lightning. The end of mankind became his end. Even the women in the episode have their place in Revelations in the references to “the whore of Babylon”. The Hotel itself with its gaudy elegance and fervent activity became the Babylon of Revelations. He tries the sixth floor door three times: 666, the number of the beast. The dynamo is God, the machinery of Armegeddon. As a playwright Strindberg is probably familiar with the ancient Greek literary device, “deux ex machina”: God from machine.

All this, again, is bound up in the mother archetype which he struggles to free himself of but ends up in the embrace of the very Furie he tries to escape.
