Thank You for Not Smoking THIS!

Secondhand smoke, first-hand sanctimony, and one exceptionally well-timed backfire

Written by Peter Mangiaracina

Complaint Public sanctimony with vehicular accompaniment

The other day, I’m walking down Fifth Avenue, happily sucking on a little cigar I had just picked up at a tobacco store. It’s a cold day, and the wind is blowing all over the place. Coming down the crowded street at a rheumatic jog is a little old lady in a pale blue running suit. She picks me out of the crowd and hobbles straight for me. When she gets about a foot from my face, she starts doing that phony accusing cough all smokers are familiar with. You know, the one that sounds like ahick, ahick. She stops dead in her tracks, plants bony knuckles on sharp hips, and shouts, “You’ll kill us all.” Just then, an old Volkswagen backfires on the crowded avenue, sending out a thick nimbus of blue smoke that all but obscures her wrinkled face. Grandma doesn’t flinch.

Secondhand Smoke. I’m sick of hearing about it, and I’m tired of getting treated like a war criminal with a pack of weapons of mass destruction in my shirt pocket.

There are people living near nuclear reactors, drinking the water, and giving birth to Siamese lizard babies. There are dizzy dorks running down Fifth Avenue in designer jogging suits, hoovering enough exhaust fumes into their lungs to punch a hole in the ozone layer. There are people swimming in the ocean where a million octopi are relieving their bowels. None of these people has to stand in a crowded, plastic, windowless, phone-booth-sized prison cell at the airport like a vacant-eyed pariah for one last cigarette before a twelve-hour flight.

Talk to any doctor, and he’ll tell you how bad smoking is for your health, and I agree. The evidence is overwhelming; there’s no denying it. Still, this secondhand smoke business is a way of controlling my free choice, and I don’t take kindly to it. Considering the number of poisonous gases released into the atmosphere every time you get into your Uncle Roger’s Range Rover, the effects of secondhand smoke are nominal at most unless you happen to be locked in the closet with a chain-smoking fiend for the better part of a decade. Time and energy would be better spent on looking for clean, renewable car fuel than on demonizing smoking.

I don’t know exactly when smoking went from being the Hollywood prop of hipsters to the stage business of degenerates. I never much cared, but now it’s leaking into my life, and—as even my pet parrot knows—I’m having none of it.

We are in the grip of a full-blown paranoid obsession about smoking, and I’ve got to tell you, this whole culture of the tobacco demon is beginning to get on my nerves. So, tobacco companies are a bunch of lying scoundrels who misled the American public, and many people have died because of their deceitful tactics.

When you demonize something, it isn’t enough that it is dangerous on a purely personal level: I smoke, I die. My choice, hand me the Zippo. That’s not enough. The guy who aims a daily Big Mac at his gullet isn’t doing you any harm unless he squirts ketchup on your chinos. He’ll die of a heart attack. He made his choice. End of story. Try to sue McDonald’s. To adequately demonize something, what you must do is make it dangerous for everyone. Enter the stupid term “secondhand smoke.”

I’m tired of being treated like a terrorist with a pack of weapons of mass destruction in my shirt pocket. If I smoke too much and have to drive around on a golf cart when I’m 80 to carry around spare oxygen tanks, well, that’s my choice, not yours, and I doubt if I’ll get that far because I’ll probably die of a heart attack way before that from the quarter pounders I eat every day.

And yet, here I am, ducking into alleyways and hiding behind garbage cans like a nicotine-fueled fugitive, all because society can’t handle a little puff of smoke. If my pet parrot could talk, he’d probably tell you I’m overreacting, but then again, he’s never been shamed by an elderly marathoner with lungs made of pure spite. The world, apparently, needs to be protected from me and my cigar more than it needs to be protected from actual toxic waste. Now, whenever I light up, I half expect a SWAT team to rappel down the nearest building, confiscate my lighter, and issue me a ticket for crimes against humanity. It’s enough to make you wonder if we’re all just one backfiring Volkswagen away from apocalypse.
